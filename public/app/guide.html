What counts as bullying

Not sure if what happened is bullying? Read this, then report it anyway if you're unsure. The guidance office will help decide.

The meaning, from the law

Bullying is any severe or repeated physical, verbal, written, electronic, or social behavior by one or more students that causes or may cause physical or emotional harm, creates a hostile school environment, violates another student's rights, or disrupts learning and school operations.

Anti-Bullying Act of 2013 (RA 10627). Getting back at someone who reports bullying, joins an investigation, or witnessed it is also prohibited.

Kinds of bullying

Verbal bullying
Name-calling, insults, mocking how someone looks, talks, or where they come from.
Physical bullying
Hitting, pushing, tripping, taking or damaging someone's things.
Social exclusion
Leaving someone out on purpose, spreading rumors, turning friends against them.
Cyberbullying
Hurtful posts, comments, edited photos, group chats made to mock someone, sharing private messages or photos.
Threats or intimidation
Making someone afraid, demanding money or favors, threatening to hurt them or post something about them.

What you can do

If it's happening to you

  • It's not your fault. You don't have to handle it alone.
  • Save proof if you can: screenshots, dates, names of people who saw it.
  • Report it here, or talk to your adviser or the guidance office.
  • If you're in danger right now, press SOS or call 911.

If you saw it happen

  • Don't join in, laugh, or share the post. That can make it worse.
  • If it's safe, stay with the person being bullied or help them leave.
  • Report what you saw. You can choose to hide your name.

If you bullied someone

  • Stop, and think about how the other person feels.
  • Talking to the guidance office can help you make things right and understand what led to it.
  • Apologizing and changing how you act matters more than what happened before.
