What counts as bullying

If you are unsure whether an incident counts as bullying, please read this guide. When in doubt, submit a report; the Guidance Office will assess it.

Definition under the law

Bullying is any severe or repeated physical, verbal, written, electronic, or social behavior by one or more students that causes or may cause physical or emotional harm, creates a hostile school environment, violates another student's rights, or disrupts learning and school operations.

Anti-Bullying Act of 2013 (RA 10627). Retaliation against anyone who reports bullying, takes part in an investigation, or witnesses bullying is also prohibited.

Kinds of bullying

Verbal bullying
Name-calling, insults, and mocking a person's appearance, speech, or origin.
Physical bullying
Hitting, pushing, tripping, or taking or damaging another person's belongings.
Social exclusion
Deliberately excluding a person, spreading rumors, or turning others against them.
Cyberbullying
Hurtful posts or comments, edited photos, group chats created to mock a person, and sharing private messages or photos without consent.
Threats or intimidation
Causing fear, demanding money or favors, or threatening to harm a person or to post something about them.

What you should do

If it is happening to you

  • It is not your fault, and you do not have to face it alone.
  • If possible, keep evidence such as screenshots, dates, and the names of witnesses.
  • Report it through LagroInAction, or speak with your adviser or the Guidance Office.
  • If you are in immediate danger, press SOS or call 911.

If you witnessed it

  • Do not join in, laugh, or share the post, as this can make the situation worse.
  • If it is safe to do so, stay with the person being bullied or help them leave the situation.
  • Report what you witnessed. You may choose to hide your name.

If you have bullied someone

  • Stop, and consider how the other person feels.
  • Speaking with the Guidance Office can help you make amends and understand what led to your actions.
  • Apologizing and changing your behavior matter more than what happened before.
